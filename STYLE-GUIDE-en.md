# XIQIQI · English Editorial Style Guide

> Version 1.0 · 2026-11-16  
> Applies to all 151 English entries, captions, source notes, and page summaries.

## 1. Voice and standard

- Write for a curious general reader, not for a translator or a Wikipedia editor.
- Informative, concrete, slightly wry; never sensational.
- Every paragraph must contain a fact, an image, or a clear question. No filler.
- Do not translate word-for-word. Rebuild the sentence in English, then check it against the Chinese facts.
- Use American spelling by default; keep proper names in their established English form.

## 2. Structure

- The English `##` section count must equal the Chinese `##` section count.
- Keep the Chinese narrative order unless a section is impossible to render in English.
- Each section: 2–5 paragraphs, 40–180 words per paragraph.
- Target 1,000–1,800 words per entry. Never below 900.
- Open with a concrete scene, object, date, or question; no “Since ancient times”.
- End on a person, object, place, or open question; no “the mystery remains” summary.

## 3. Sentences

- Vary length: at least one long sentence (25+ words) per paragraph; mix with short sentences.
- One action or one judgment per sentence. Do not stack three facts into one clause chain.
- Prefer active voice. Use passive only when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- Avoid: “It is worth noting”, “It is important to understand”, “In today’s world”, “This raises questions”.
- No three-item parallel lists for rhythm. No “not only … but also” chains.
- Do not start sentences with “Additionally”, “Moreover”, “Furthermore” more than once per section.

## 4. Facts and attribution

- Every hard fact (date, name, number, place, quote) must be traceable to S1–S3.
- Quotes must be real, attributed, and dated; never invent or paraphrase inside quotation marks.
- Separate legend, eyewitness account, institutional finding, and editorial inference.
- When sources disagree, say so: “Two versions circulate; the 1785 treaty text supports the first.”
- No “scientists have proven” without a named institution and year.

## 5. Proper nouns and terms

- Use `GLOSSARY.csv` for every recurring name, place, institution, object, and discipline term.
- First mention: English name only; do not put Chinese in parentheses in the English text.
- Do not invent translations for local names. If no established English form exists, keep the romanized local name and explain it once.
- Consistency over elegance: the same entity must have the same spelling across all 151 entries.

## 6. Numbers, dates, units

- Dates: `16 September 1850`; decades: `the 1850s`; centuries: `the nineteenth century`.
- Numbers: Arabic numerals for measurements, dates, statistics; spell out only at sentence start.
- Units: metric first, imperial in parentheses only when the source uses imperial.
- Ranges: `1843–1844`, `1,200–1,500`.

## 7. Captions

- Single italic line: `*Type: date, creator, license (source). Description.*`
- Type must be one of: Historical photograph / Site photograph / Historical artwork / Reconstruction / Original graphic.
- Reconstruction must say “not a historical photograph”.
- Original graphic must say “not a precise map” or “not a historical artwork”.
- Every caption must include creator, date, license, and source.

## 8. Titles and headings

- Section headings: 2–6 words, concrete, no clickbait.
- Do not reuse the same heading twice in one entry.
- Headings should promise what the section delivers; no “Background” or “More” unless truly generic.
- No numbering unless the entry is a strict timeline.

## 9. Machine-translation cleanup checklist

Before an entry can be marked `native_reviewed`:

- [ ] No literal Chinese syntax: “the boat was made to turn back”, “people have no way to know”.
- [ ] No wrong concrete nouns: “canteen” for kettle, “dysentery” for hysteria, “breed” for snowman monkey.
- [ ] No untranslated names: Nidland, Metz River, Himalayanan, Hipton, Monlon.
- [ ] No duplicated paragraphs or repeated section headings.
- [ ] No Chinese characters, full-width punctuation, or `、`/`。`.
- [ ] No “Waterpot War” when the entry is “Kettle War”.
- [ ] All numbers and dates match the Chinese text.
- [ ] Read aloud once; fix any sentence that cannot be said in one breath.

## 10. Review states

- `machine`: raw MT, not acceptable for publication.
- `edited`: human/AI line edit, facts checked against Chinese, not yet native sign-off.
- `reviewed`: English editor has read the whole entry against sources.
- `native_reviewed`: native-level editor has signed the entry; publication gate B passed.
